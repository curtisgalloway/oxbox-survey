# qwen3.8-27b:free is refused by its only provider's shared pool while its uptime reads 100 percent

**What happened** — the third of the four free rows OpenRouter added since the
2026-09-10 catalog never ran. Two attempts at the zero-defect control, 83
seconds apart, both refused with a 429 before anything was sent. The row has
exactly one endpoint, and OpenRouter reports that endpoint healthy with 100
percent uptime over the preceding 30 minutes.

## Evidence

```
oxbox-send: HTTP 429: {"error":{"message":"Provider returned error","code":429,
"metadata":{"raw":"qwen/qwen3.8-27b:free is temporarily rate-limited upstream.
Please retry shortly, or add your own key to accumulate your rate limits",
"provider_name":"ModelRun","is_byok":false,"provider_error_code":"429",
"limit_source":"upstream_provider_shared_pool"}}}
```

Identical on both attempts, 01-56-24Z and 01-57-47Z. Instant, ~1 s wall each,
no route assigned in either `status.json`.

The row's endpoint list, fetched immediately after the second refusal:

```
endpoints: 1
   modelrun/fp4   ModelRun   status 0   uptime_last_30m 100
```

One provider, no alternative to route to, marked healthy, at 100 percent
uptime for the window that contains both refusals.

Stopped after two. A shared free pool is a common resource and hammering it is
how the next caller gets refused; `limit_source` names it
`upstream_provider_shared_pool`, not a per-key limit, so a third attempt would
have been someone else's problem as much as ours.

## So what

**Uptime is not availability on a free row.** The 100 percent figure is the
provider's endpoint answering *somebody*; it says nothing about whether it will
answer you, because the free tier is a shared pool and the limit is on the pool
rather than on the key. A reader picking a free model from a catalog that
publishes uptime will read 100 percent and conclude the row is reachable. It
was not reachable twice in a row here.

**One endpoint means no failover to buy.** The survey's paid entries pin
several routes with `allow_fallbacks: false` precisely so a refusal is visible.
This row has no such choice: `modelrun/fp4` is the only thing serving it, so a
saturated pool is a total outage for that model, not a slow path. That is a
structural property of the listing, visible in the catalog before any request,
and it belongs in the limitation column beside the model.

The disqualifier is `rate_limited` and it self-clears: the next run-backed row
for this model dated on or after today clears it, per the 2026-09-06 rule. It
is recorded on two attempts over 83 seconds on one evening, which is a
reachability fact and not a measurement of the pool's behavior over time. This
says nothing about the model's quality — it never saw a request.

Worth a retry on another day before the next issue, since a `:free` row that
cannot be reached is not a recommendation whatever it would have scored.
