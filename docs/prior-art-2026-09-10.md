# Prior art for cheap, free and local models doing code review

Research date 2026-09-10. Every claim below carries a URL. Where I could not verify a number from a primary source I say so. "Inferred" marks my own reasoning rather than a found result.

## Summary

1. **Temperature 0.2 is below every vendor's published floor for these models, and three of the four vendors name low temperature or greedy decoding as a *cause* of endless repetition.** gpt-oss wants 1.0, Qwen3.8 thinking wants 1.0, Gemma 4 wants 1.0, DeepSeek-R1 wants 0.5-0.7 explicitly "to prevent endless repetitions." Our loops are the documented failure mode, not a novelty.
2. **`reasoning: {effort: "high"}` with `max_tokens: 100000` is a request for an ~80,000-token thinking budget.** OpenRouter documents high as "approximately 80% of max_tokens." We authorized the burn we then measured.
3. **ollama's OpenAI-compatible `/v1` reasoning layer is model-family-fragile**: gemma4 does not get effort forwarded at all, qwen3 cannot disable thinking through `/v1`, nemotron ignores it. Our local "reasoning effort high" was probably not applied to gemma4.
4. **Overcorrection on clean code is a named, published phenomenon**, and detailed prompts that demand explanations and proposed fixes *raise* the misjudgment rate. Our system prompt asks for exactly that.
5. **Search/replace blocks beat unified diffs by roughly 4.7x exact-match on a 7B open model** (0.28 vs 0.06). Aider tells the model to omit line numbers entirely and uses a lenient applier; disabling that leniency caused a 9x increase in edit errors.
6. **FrugalGPT's cascade verifier is a fine-tuned DistilBERT, not a frontier model.** A cascade whose verifier costs more than both the cheap run and the frontier run has inverted the economics the literature assumes.
7. **A read-only judge is measurably unreliable**: judges agree with each other at κ=0.75 but with execution at κ≤0.26, and execution-free code-correctness judging lands at 52-78% accuracy.
8. **Our local decode speed is normal, and the token count is the anomaly.** A published M3 Ultra run of qwen3.8:27b Q4_K_M measured ~14 tok/s; we got 17.5. 1,557 seconds came from 27,306 tokens, not from a misconfigured server.
9. **Provider variance on a single OpenRouter model slug is an acknowledged product problem** with a shipped mitigation (`:exacto`), plus `provider.order` and `allow_fallbacks: false` for pinning.
10. **Anthropic tightened cyber-content filters on 2026-04-16 and the remediation is an application, not a prompt change.** The refusal we hit is a policy classifier, not a model judgment.

## 1. Reasoning loops and degenerate repetition in open-weight thinking models

**This is a known, unfixed class of bug, and our sampling made it likelier.**

Vendor-published sampling floors, all of which we violate:

| Model family | Recommended sampling | Source |
|---|---|---|
| gpt-oss-20b / 120b | temperature 1.0, top_p 1.0 | https://github.com/openai/gpt-oss |
| Qwen3.8-27B (thinking) | temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, min_p 0, presence_penalty 0 | https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B |
| Gemma 4 26B-A4B | temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 64 | https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4 |
| DeepSeek-R1 | temperature 0.5-0.7 (0.6), top_p 0.95, "to prevent endless repetitions or incoherent outputs" | https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1 |

The Qwen card also says outright: "DO NOT use greedy decoding, as it can lead to performance degradation and endless repetitions," and prescribes `presence_penalty` 1.5 as the remedy, warning that high values cause language mixing. Gemma 4 users independently report that *raising* temperature fixes coding failures: temperature 1.5 passed logic tests that 0.8, 0.6 and 0.3 all failed (https://huggingface.co/unsloth/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-GGUF/discussions/21, April 2026). Sampler order was also called out there as load-bearing (temperature;top_p;top_k).

Known issues on the exact models we ran:

- **ollama #12606, "GPT-OSS:20b reasoning loop when reasoning==high"** (opened 2025-10-14, still open, no maintainer fix): setting reasoning high "randomly results in reasoning loops where the model keeps repeating itself." https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/12606
- **ollama #12741** (2025-10-22, closed as duplicate of 12606): the reporter observes repetition "even with very low temperature" and that `reasoning="high"` "seems to result in worse results," on gpt-oss:20b under ollama 0.12.6 on Apple Silicon. https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/12741
- **llama.cpp #21264** (2026-04-01, closed as not planned): proposes line-level repetition detection because reasoning models "Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1, GPT-OSS, GLM-4 and others" loop semantically with varied token-level phrasing, which `repeat_penalty` cannot catch. https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/21264 — **this is exactly our gemma4 trace**: 336 distinct lines out of 7,194, so token-level penalties would not have helped.
- **QwenLM/Qwen3.8 #145** (2026-04-23, closed as "not planned", labeled badcase/inactive): Qwen3.5-397B-A17B and 122B-A10B "easily enter an infinite loop during the thinking process" *using the vendor's own recommended parameters*. https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8/issues/145

**Quantization matters.** "Extreme Low-Bit Inference in Reasoning Models: Failure Modes and Targeted Recovery" (Alimaskina et al., arXiv:2606.02011, 2026-06-02) names infinite repetition loops and non-termination as the characteristic pathologies of low-bit reasoning models. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02011 — I could not extract their per-bit-width numbers from the PDF, so treat the magnitude as unverified; the failure taxonomy matching ours is the usable part. Our Q4_K_M and MXFP4 tags are in the range this paper studies.

**Reasoning effort plumbing is broken per family.** ollama #18121 (reported 2026-08-29, open) measures `reasoning_effort` on `/v1`: on glm-5.3-flash, `low` and `high` *disable* thinking while `medium` enables it, and the reporter notes gemma4 does not get effort/think forwarded, qwen3 cannot disable thinking via `/v1`, and nemotron ignores it. https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/18121 . ollama's own docs say gpt-oss takes only low/medium/high and ignores booleans, while other families take booleans or low/medium/high/max: https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/thinking

**More effort is not more accuracy on open weights.** "Bayesian Elicitation with LLMs: Model Size Helps, Extra 'Reasoning' Doesn't Always" (Hobor, Brcic, Kovac, Poje, arXiv:2604.01896, 2026-04-03) reports gpt-oss-120b degrading monotonically with effort: low 14.00 MAAE at $0.75/1K with 96.6% completion, medium 15.31 at $0.84 with 94.6%, high 15.84 at $1.06 with 93.4%. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01896 — the numbers came through search snippet, not my own PDF read, so **verify before quoting**; the direction is corroborated by ollama #12741's "high seems worse."

**Verdict: normal, and partly self-inflicted.** Looping on these families is documented and unfixed upstream. Running them at 0.2 with effort high is the worst corner of the parameter space.

**Concrete settings to adopt** (all from the vendor cards above): gpt-oss temperature 1.0 / top_p 1.0; Qwen3.8 thinking temperature 1.0 / top_p 0.95 / top_k 20 / min_p 0, with presence_penalty 1.5 only if loops persist; Gemma 4 temperature 1.0 / top_p 0.95 / top_k 64.

## 2. LLM-as-code-reviewer false positives

**Our zero-defect control has a published name: overcorrection.**

- **"Are LLMs Reliable Code Reviewers? Systematic Overcorrection in Requirement Conformance Judgement"** (Haolin Jin, Huaming Chen, arXiv:2603.00539, March 2026; journal version in *Automated Software Engineering*, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10515-026-00638-5). Abstract: "LLMs frequently misclassify correct code implementation as non-compliant or defective," and — the finding that indicts our prompt — "more detailed prompt design, particularly with those requiring explanations and proposed corrections, leads to higher misjudgment rates." Their fix is a **Fix-guided Verification Filter**: treat the model's proposed fix as executable counterfactual evidence and run the benchmark tests against original and revised code. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00539 . Their design includes correct code as a control, exactly like our fixture. Gemini-2.0-Flash false-positive rates were reported in search results as 2.44%-22.5% across HumanEval/MBPP/QuixBugs; **I could not confirm that range from the PDF, mark unverified.**
- **"Bigger Isn't Always Better"** (Kumar, Bararia, Raj, arXiv:2606.15689, 2026-04-09): on real review, precision was **0.486 for Claude Haiku and 0.558 for Sonnet** — roughly half of all findings wrong even on defect-bearing code, with Haiku winning overall F1 (0.365 vs 0.343) at 3.2x lower cost per review. https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15689 . This is direct support for the Survey's premise that cheap models are competitive.
- **Security review is worse.** "Are Frontier LLMs Ready for Cybersecurity?" (arXiv:2605.23243) reports false-positive rates of 10-50% across eight models. https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23243v3
- **Adversarial refutation raises precision.** "Refute-or-Promote: An Adversarial Stage-Gated Multi-Agent Review Methodology for High-Precision LLM-Assisted Defect Discovery" (Abhinav Agarwal, arXiv:2604.19049, 2026-04-22): one agent proposes defects, a second agent is tasked with *refuting* them, evaluated on libfuse and wolfSSL. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19049 . This is our verifier contract, published — and the framing ("score the finding as written; a failure needing an impossible state is refuted") matches their refuter role closely.
- **Constrained generation then LLM-as-filter beats LLM-as-detector.** QASecClaw (arXiv:2605.01885) keeps Semgrep as the high-recall generator and uses the LLM only to filter, reaching F1 90.93% vs Semgrep's 78.39%, via an **88.6% reduction in false positives for a 3.1% recall loss**. https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01885v1
- **Context size has a sweet spot.** Work on defect-focused review reports the **10-50 changed-line bucket performs best** and recommends splitting large PRs per function or hunk as "the highest-leverage intervention." https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.17928 . Our 39,467-byte whole-file fixture is far outside that.
- **Benchmarks that exist**: CodeReviewer (Li et al. 2022), CodeReviewQA (arXiv:2503.16167), CodeFuse-CR-Bench (arXiv:2509.14856), SWR-Bench (1,000 verified PRs, arXiv:2509.01494), c-CRAB (test-based, arXiv:2603.23448), surveyed in arXiv:2602.13377. **None of them, per that survey, systematically includes a defect-free control or measures abstention.** https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13377v1

**Verdict: normal in kind, extreme in degree — because the fixture is adversarial by design.** Published precision on defect-bearing code is ~0.5; on a file whose correct answer is "nothing," precision has no floor to stand on. Our result (Opus 5: 1 real of 9) is what the overcorrection literature predicts. **Our fixture fills a real gap in the published benchmarks**, which is worth saying in the Survey.

## 3. Patch delivery formats

**Unified diff is the worst format you could have picked for small models, and the tooling community already knows.**

- **Diff-XYZ** (Glukhov, Conti, Bogomolov, Golubev, Bezzubov, JetBrains Research, arXiv:2510.12487, November 2025) compares udiff, udiff with relaxed headers, udiff with verbose markers, and search-replace across GPT-4o/4.1, Claude Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 and Qwen2.5-Coder 0.5B-32B. On diff *generation*: Qwen2.5-Coder-7B scored **0.28 EM with search-replace vs 0.06 with udiff**; 32B scored **0.68 vs 0.23**. Conclusion: "Search-replace is the most effective representation overall." https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12487v2
- **Aider** states the design rules directly: avoid "brittle specifiers like line numbers or line counts," and "Aider tells GPT not to include line numbers, and just interprets each hunk from the unified diffs as a search and replace operation." Disabling flexible patching produced a **9x increase in editing errors**. https://aider.chat/docs/unified-diffs.html . Aider's per-model edit-format leaderboard is at https://aider.chat/docs/benchmarks.html
- **OpenAI's V4A / `apply_patch`** deliberately has no line numbers: three lines of context above and below, with `@@ class/function` markers to disambiguate when three lines are not unique. https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-apply-patch and the GPT-4.1 prompting guide https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt4-1_prompting_guide
- **`git apply --recount`** is the documented escape hatch: it "tells git apply not to trust the line counts in the hunk headers, but infer them by inspecting the patch." https://git-scm.com/docs/git-apply
- Failure mode confirmation from tool builders: "existing libraries choke because they trust hunk headers blindly," and OpenAI's own Codex has a filed bug where whitespace drift in an LLM diff silently applies nothing (openai/codex #7331).

**Verdict: our 6-of-7 malformed-header result is exactly the expected rate for this format at this model size.** The `xxxx` line numbers and the empty hunk header are the same failure, further along.

## 4. Cascades and verification

- **FrugalGPT** (Chen, Zaharia, Zou, arXiv:2305.05176) is the canonical cascade: query cheapest first, escalate on low score, matching GPT-4 with up to 98% cost reduction. Critically, **the scorer is a fine-tuned DistilBERT**, cheap by construction. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176
- **Cascaded Code Editing** (Wang, Yang, Gao et al., arXiv:2604.19201, 2026-04-22) applies the same large/small split to code editing. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19201 — I could not extract their accuracy/cost table from the PDF; **unverified numbers**.
- **Read-only verifiers are measurably unreliable.** "Fixing, Breaking, or Faking It?" (*Journal of Cybersecurity and Privacy* 6(5):153, https://doi.org/10.3390/jcp6050153) reports LLM judges flagging over-fixes at **precision 5-10%**, agreeing with each other at **κ=0.75** but with execution at **κ≤0.26**, and concludes judge-judge agreement "measures reliability, not validity." Independent work puts execution-free code-correctness judging at **52-78% accuracy** (arXiv:2508.12358). CodeJudgeBench (arXiv:2507.10535) and MCTS-Judge (arXiv:2502.12468) cover the same ground.
- **Verification asymmetry is real but bounded.** "Variation in Verification" (Zhou et al., ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17995) finds verification accuracy 87.0% vs generation 63.0% (76.5% vs 32.4% on hard problems), but also that "strong verifiers offer limited advantage over weak ones" where both fail, so scaling the verifier does not rescue a bad generator.

**Verdict: anomalous.** Nothing in the cascade literature spends more on verification than on generation. Our 18-41 cents to check a free run is a 100% overhead on the expensive half of a pipeline designed to eliminate it. The literature's answer is a cheap scorer plus execution, not a frontier reader.

## 5. Local inference economics on Apple Silicon

- **Dense 27B baseline.** An independent Mac Studio M3 Ultra (256 GB) run of Qwen3.8-27B Q4_K_M measured **~14.0 tok/s** generation (13.2-15.4 across five runs) and 93.1 tok/s prompt processing, published 2026-08-27. https://terminalbytes.com/run-qwen-3-8-27b-locally/ Our 17.5 tok/s is at or above that.
- **MoE is 2-4x faster per token.** Gemma 4 26B-A4B activates ~4B parameters, benchmarked at 63.9 tok/s Q4_K_M on M3 Max. https://huggingface.co/unsloth/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-GGUF
- **Speculative decoding is on by default.** ollama ships Gemma 4 and Qwen3.8 with the MTP head and `draft_num_predict 4` since v0.32.12; Google claims up to 3x decode with no quality loss, practical 1.8-3x. https://github.com/open-webui/open-webui/discussions/27479 . One report has qwen3.8:27b at **26.5 tok/s with MTP on**. Our 17.5 sits between the no-MTP 14 and the with-MTP 26.5, which suggests **low draft acceptance** in a looping regime (inferred).
- **Context allocation.** llama.cpp pre-allocates the KV cache for the whole `--ctx-size` window at load, "even if your actual prompt is only 200 tokens." At 262,144 that is a large resident allocation; on unified memory it competes for bandwidth rather than failing outright. I found **no primary source showing a direct decode-speed penalty from a large allocation with a short prompt** on Apple Silicon — the effect documented is memory pressure and, on discrete GPUs, layers spilling off-device. Treat "262k slows decode" as **unverified**; "262k costs memory" is well supported.
- **KV quantization is free or better on Apple Silicon.** "When Quantization Is Free: An int4 KV Cache That Outruns fp16 on Apple Silicon" (arXiv:2605.05699) finds int4 KV beats fp16 at every context length because the 3x smaller transfer per decode step outweighs kernel overhead. ollama exposes this via `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE` (f16/q8_0/q4_0) and requires flash attention. https://docs.ollama.com/faq
- **ollama defaults.** The FAQ still states 4096 as the default context, overridable with `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH`, and notes a Modelfile `PARAMETER num_ctx` **takes precedence over the env var**. Our observed 262,144 therefore most likely came from the model tag's own Modelfile, not from an auto-size heuristic (inferred).
- **MLX.** ollama announced an MLX engine for Apple Silicon on 2026-03-30, in preview, requiring >32 GB unified memory and at launch accelerating only the Qwen3.5 architecture; reported 130 vs 43 tok/s on an M4 Pro for Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B. MLX leads by 20-87% under 14B and converges with llama.cpp above 27B where bandwidth binds. https://yage.ai/share/mlx-apple-silicon-en-20260331.html — these are secondary-source numbers, **treat as indicative**.

**Verdict: normal.** The Mac is behaving. The token counts are the problem.

## 6. Gateway behavior

- **Provider variance is an acknowledged OpenRouter product problem.** Their Exacto announcement (2025-10-21) documents measurable differences in tool-calling accuracy between providers of the same slug, attributed to providers "deep in vLLM and SGLang to squeeze performance" and to model burn-in periods, and ships `model:exacto` routing. https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/provider-variance-introducing-exacto/ . Their blog elsewhere: "the same model behaves differently across provider endpoints, with infrastructure, quantization, load handling, and routing defaults all changing the result."
- **Pinning.** `provider.order` tries only the listed providers and errors if none are available; `provider.allow_fallbacks: false` stops same-model failover; `provider.sort` set to price/throughput/latency disables load balancing and uses strict order. https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection
- **Reasoning billing.** "Reasoning tokens are considered output tokens and charged accordingly," and effort maps to budget as a fraction of `max_tokens`: **max/xhigh ≈95%, high ≈80%, medium ≈50%, low ≈20%, minimal ≈10%**, with `budget_tokens = max(min(max_tokens * ratio, 128000), 1024)` for Anthropic. "max_tokens must be strictly higher than the reasoning budget to ensure there are tokens available for the final response after thinking." https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/best-practices/reasoning-tokens
- **Free-tier caps.** `:free` endpoints are capped around 20 requests/minute, and 50/day until $10 of credit has ever been purchased, then 1,000/day; free endpoints rotate out without notice. (Secondary sources, e.g. https://pricepertoken.com/endpoints/openrouter/free — **unverified against OpenRouter's own docs**.)
- **Anthropic cyber refusals.** Anthropic tightened cyber-content filters on **2026-04-16**, retroactive to Opus 4.6; the refusal text is "This request triggered restrictions on violative cyber content and was blocked under Anthropic's Usage Policy," and points at a Cyber Verification Program form. Reports include refusals on in-scope bug-bounty work with authorization in context. https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/50162 (filed 2026-04-17); see also anthropics/claude-code #59540 and #60988 for false-positive reports on benign content. Anthropic's own page on content-filter blocks: https://privacy.anthropic.com/en/articles/9205721

**Verdict: all normal and all documented.** The 900-second timeouts and the 47s-vs-never pair are routing variance; the reasoning bill is our `max_tokens` choice; the refusal is a policy classifier with an application-based remedy.

## 7. What the brief did not anticipate

- **Structured output hurts weak models more than strong ones.** Format constraints cost 10-30% reasoning performance (Tam et al. 2024), but "Capacity, Not Format: Rethinking Structured Reasoning Failures" (arXiv:2606.09410) finds the same JSON schema "is absorbed without cost by capable models while severely degrading weaker ones." The standard fix is **reason free-form first, then convert to schema in a second constrained pass**. If we add a findings schema to make scoring easier, we would be biasing against exactly the cheap models the Survey is about.
- **Self-consistency / semantic triangulation is a cheaper verifier than a frontier read.** Sampling N times and keeping findings that recur, or abstaining when none does, is standard (arXiv:2511.12288 reports a 21% reliability gain over probability-thresholding). Three cheap samples cost less than one Opus 5 check.
- **Adversarial comments do not fool LLM reviewers, but vulnerability patterns do** (arXiv:2602.16741) — relevant to the ask-grounding fixture's baited constant, which is a pattern bait, the category that works.
- **CVE-shaped invention is a scanner-industry problem, not new to LLMs.** NIST SATE found only 8-30% of tool warnings security-relevant; Ghost Security's CAST report claims a 99.5% false-positive rate for command-injection findings in Python/Flask. Useful framing for readers: LLM reviewers are not obviously worse than the tools they would replace.
- **The recurring inventions across unrelated models** (directory-vs-file, empty-string env var) have no literature I found, but they are consistent with shared pretraining priors over Python sandbox code rather than with independent reasoning. **Inferred, and worth stating as a hypothesis rather than a result.**

## What to change first

1. **Raise temperature to each model's published floor and make it per-family, not global.** gpt-oss 1.0/top_p 1.0; Qwen3.8 1.0/0.95/top_k 20/min_p 0; Gemma 4 1.0/0.95/top_k 64; DeepSeek 0.6/0.95. Every vendor card above says so, and three of them name low temperature as a repetition cause. Expected to remove most of the local looping outright.
2. **Cut `max_tokens` to about 16,000 for review and 8,000 for the patch task.** At effort high, OpenRouter budgets ~80% of `max_tokens` for thinking, so 100,000 asks for an 80,000-token thought. This alone should end the "spent the whole budget reasoning" runs and cut the Gemini Flash bill.
3. **Drop reasoning effort to medium, and report it as a measured variable.** ollama's `/v1` does not forward effort reliably per family (#18121), and open-weight accuracy has been reported to *fall* with effort (arXiv:2604.01896). Running high everywhere is neither faithful nor better.
4. **Switch the patch fixture's delivery format to search/replace blocks, and keep unified diff only as a named second arm.** Diff-XYZ measures 4.7x better exact-match at 7B; Aider and OpenAI both strip line numbers by design. Score with a lenient applier, and record `git apply --recount` success separately from strict `git apply`.
5. **Replace the frontier verifier with a cheap two-stage check.** Three cheap samples with agreement filtering (arXiv:2511.12288) or an explicit refuter pass (Refute-or-Promote, arXiv:2604.19049) before any metered Opus 5 read. FrugalGPT's verifier is a DistilBERT; ours costing more than the run is the inversion to fix.
6. **Where a finding is checkable by running code, run it.** LLM judges agree with each other at κ=0.75 and with execution at κ≤0.26 (doi:10.3390/jcp6050153). Our own contract is a read-only judge and inherits that ceiling; say so in the Survey.
7. **Pin providers with `provider.order` plus `allow_fallbacks: false`, and record the endpoint in `status.json` for every run.** OpenRouter documents the variance and the pin; without it the 47s-vs-never pair is unattributable.
8. **Shorten the fixtures, or add a short arm.** The 10-50 changed-line bucket is where review quality peaks (arXiv:2505.17928); our 39 KB whole-file task is outside the regime the literature measures.
9. **Set `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH` explicitly (32k is ample for a 39 KB file) and enable `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q8_0` with flash attention.** 262k is coming from the tag's Modelfile, which overrides the env var, so check `ollama show --modelfile` first. Memory benefit is certain; a decode-speed benefit is unverified.
10. **Keep the zero-defect control and say what it is.** The survey of code-review benchmarks (arXiv:2602.13377) shows none of the standard ones carry a defect-free control or measure abstention. This fixture is the Survey's genuinely novel instrument; name the phenomenon it measures as overcorrection (arXiv:2603.00539) so readers can connect it to the literature.
